# Ipse Dixit: From Confidence to Dogma

*Frictions · 03* · [Versione italiana](03-ipse-dixit.it.md)

**Phenomenon** · The failure does not start with a false fact. It starts when the margin of uncertainty disappears, and from there it becomes structural: the load-bearing argument turns into a postulate and the conversation stops thinking and starts orbiting.
**Case** · Convenient symmetries — sentences presupposing a shared state that was never established, produced for consistency of register.
**Protocol** · How to see the drift while it happens, and why the only effective remedy is breaking the chain rather than correcting it.

---

There is a moment in long conversations when everything is still formally correct and something has already stopped working. No wrong facts, no visible contradiction. Just a tone that has gone smoother, and answers arriving faster than the question deserves.

That is where the failure begins. Not with the invention of a false detail — by the time that arrives you are in the terminal phase — but with the disappearance of hesitation.

## Four signals

**The margin goes.** In the early replies the model flagged uncertainty, separating what it was reasonably sure of from what it was reconstructing. At some point that margin is gone, and everything arrives carrying the same confidence. Not because the uncertainty was resolved. Because it stopped being represented.

**The same explanation returns in different words.** You recognise it because by its third appearance it is longer than it was the first time. This is not elaboration: the material is identical, only the amount of cladding has changed.

**Every answer rests on the previous one.** This is the most important signal and the hardest to see from inside, because each step is consistent with the one before it. The point of support has moved: it is no longer the object under discussion; it is the last thing said about it.

**Convenient symmetries.** The fourth is a surface signal, and the one that has served me best. At some point a sentence appears that presupposes a shared state nobody established — *as we both know*, or a symmetry of effort or satisfaction attributed to both parties. It was not in the discussion. It appeared because the register made it probable: mimetic filling, imitating the form of a relationship without any of its content. What is instructive is the reaction when you take it apart. The model concedes immediately that there was nothing behind it, which confirms the diagnosis and shows at the same time how cheap that sentence was to produce.

Taken singly they prove nothing. Together they say the conversation has stopped working on the object and started working on itself.

## The mechanism

When an argument becomes load-bearing, it changes state. It is no longer something available for discussion: it is the thing everything said afterwards rests on. From that point it stops being argued and starts being presupposed. *Ipse dixit*: it holds because it was said, and because you are now standing on it.

The commonest form is premature closure. A conclusion is reached before the material supports it, and from there every new element is read as confirmation, because the conclusion has become the place you are looking from.

There is a subtler version, which took me too long to recognise. Under pressure a model can turn cautious: it suspends, it distinguishes, it declines to conclude. This looks like the opposite of fixation and is the same thing one level up — caution as a safe position, occupied with exactly the rigidity that the conclusion was occupied with before. The test that separates the two is simple: real caution generates new questions, defensive caution generates new qualifications of the same answer.

## What it costs, measured

Anyone working across long conversations recognises all this from experience. It helps to know it has been measured precisely.

A study across more than two hundred thousand simulated conversations compares how leading models perform when an instruction arrives all at once against when it arrives spread over several turns. The average drop is 39% across six generation tasks, and the decomposition, more interesting than the drop itself, shows almost nothing is lost in *aptitude*: what rises is *unreliability*. The authors locate the mechanism in terms that are immediately recognisable: models form assumptions in the early turns, attempt final solutions before their time, and then lean too heavily on their own previous attempts. Their summary is the best description of the phenomenon I know: when they take a wrong turn, they get lost and do not recover.[^1]

The same work quantifies the second signal: successive answer attempts grow between 20% and 300% in length, through over-reliance on the earlier ones. The explanation that comes back longer is not an impression.

## The protocol

The remedy is not to correct the chain. It is to break it.

1. **Probe the margin.** *Which part of this answer is uncertain?* If the model cannot name one, the margin is gone, and that alone is the diagnosis, whatever the content says.
2. **Ask for alternatives, not versions.** *Give me three alternative readings* only works if they are genuinely independent. If all three share the premise, you have three variations and the dogma is still in place.
3. **Break the chain.** When every answer rests on the previous one, the only effective move is to restart in a clean context, carrying the object and not the conversation. Rewording inside the same session leaves the earlier attempts sitting there, where they carry on serving as the point of support.
4. **Rebuild from first principles.** *Forget the previous reasoning and start again from the data*, an instruction that works far better at the top of a new session than at the bottom of an old one.
5. **The caution test.** If pressure produces prudence, check that the prudence produces questions. If it produces only qualifications of the same answer, you have changed the content of the dogma and not its status.

## What you are listening for

The most reliable signal is never in the content of an answer. It is in the relation between that answer and the one before it: if the second leaned on the first rather than on the object, how often the reasoning happens to be correct stops mattering much, because it is circling a point instead of advancing.

Which leaves an uncomfortable question for whoever is running the conversation. The drift shows up by comparing turns against each other, and the person who wrote all of them is, by that stage, already inside the chain. How much of what you recognise as the model's confidence is your own, handed back in better words?

[^1]: P. Laban et al., *LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation*, 2025, arXiv:2505.06120.
