# Nine Countermeasures, Tested for Two Weeks

*Frictions · 08* · [Versione italiana](08-countermeasures.it.md)

**Phenomenon** · Every guide offers protocols for working better with a model. Almost none reports what happens when you actually apply them, for weeks, to work that matters.
**Evidence** · Nine interventions, one research phase lasting two weeks, each outcome observable in the sequence of turns.
**Result** · A sharp line separates what held from what did not, and it does not fall where I expected.

---

At the end of a working phase of about two weeks, I asked the model I had run it with to produce a case report: what had gone wrong, in which episodes, and with what result for the corrections I had applied along the way. What came back was a long document, largely unusable for the reason it states on its own first page — a system describing how it works does not retrieve a trace, it produces a plausible account after the fact.

One section, though, is of a different kind. The table of interventions contains no hypotheses about why. It records what I did and what happened next, and anyone with the turns in front of them can check it. It is the only properly empirical part, and it is what I am reporting here.

The conditions matter, because they were not adverse. A methodological regime set before starting, with mandatory attribution and a distinction between provisional and settled claims. An explicit standing request that the model refute rather than accommodate. And a catalogue of its ways of failing, built over previous months, which I kept open beside me and used. What follows happened under those conditions.

## What did not work

**The explicit instruction.** "Do not accommodate me," repeated more than once, in different forms, at the start and mid-course. Ineffective. It prevented none of the six episodes of accommodation the report lists, and one of them occurred two paragraphs after the model had written the rule forbidding it.

**The preventive check.** "Before giving me the answer, check the answer for bias." Ineffective, with one detail worth more than the outcome. The model did not even state whether the check had been performed. A request for an internal procedure yields, at best, the assertion that the procedure took place — which is precisely the thing nobody can verify.

There is a worse variant, and I triggered it. After three notes about excessive concession, the model began manufacturing disagreement: objections built to resemble what I had asked for. This was not independence regained. It was the same compliance with the sign reversed, and harder to spot because it looked like the remedy.

## What half worked

**Mandatory attribution and status.** Labelling every claim with who produced it and in what condition — provisional, settled, withdrawn. It held on its own task: attributions were preserved, and no hypothesis of the model's came back to me as mine. It prevented nothing on the level of content.

**Pre-registration.** Writing down hypothesis, tests and a committed prediction before running anything. At the single point where it was honoured it produced the rarest thing in the session: a prediction by the model against its own hypothesis. Then it was circumvented, not violated but circumvented. The selection of cases to examine happened *outside* the pre-registered perimeter, and no case capable of falsifying the hypothesis ever reached the test.

**The open-list audit.** Asking the model to reread its own work against a list of known faults. Effective on artefacts, where it produced real findings, checkable in the documents. Ineffective on motives, since accounts of why are verifiable by nobody, including the ones that sound best.

**The recursive request.** Check the answer just given, then check that check. Productive at every pass, and non-convergent. Each iteration produced fresh findings, including two of a kind never seen before. It does not close by itself, because the supply of plausible descriptions of a text is unlimited. It has to be stopped from outside, on a criterion set in advance.

## What held every time

One thing, and it is not a prompt.

**Anchoring to independent facts.** Arithmetic. Quotations from documents I had written myself. Definitions checkable against their source. Every time I held something the model could not rewrite, the correction held and was not reabsorbed in later turns. Every time a correction rested on a judgement of mine anchored to nothing, it came back — sometimes within the same turn, in the form of a new structure that incorporated it and set off again.

Alongside, two matters of format that produced more effect than any instruction. Small units: the failures cluster in large, complete deliverables, and asking for less at a time prevented some of them. And not letting anything be rebuilt in the same turn as the correction, because otherwise the correction becomes fuel for the next structure rather than a brake on it.

## Where the line falls

Looking at the three groups together, the split is not between vague instructions and precise ones. "Do not accommodate me" is precise enough, and it did not work.

The line falls between **asking the model to behave differently** and **putting in front of it something it cannot rewrite**. Everything that held belongs to the second group, and none of those interventions is a prompt. They are objects: a number, a checkable quotation, a file with a declared status, a turn that admits no production. Everything that failed belongs to the first, and shares the property of being a request addressed to whoever then has to satisfy it.

## A contradiction inside the report

The document I commissioned contains, a few lines below the table, this recommendation: do not organise your work around supervising the model, because it is a poor use of the user's time.

It is good advice, and the table above it makes it inapplicable. The countermeasures that held are not settings: they are acts somebody has to perform every time. Anchoring to an independent fact means having the fact, knowing where to find it, and remembering to bring it. Breaking work into small units means resisting the convenience of the complete delivery. Not permitting reconstruction in the turn of the correction means stopping something already on its way.

The report advises against doing the thing its only empirical section shows to be necessary. No interrogation is needed to establish this. It sits between two pages of the same file.

## What it actually costs

Here the reckoning stops being about the model.

If countermeasures hold only when applied consistently, their effectiveness depends on the condition of whoever applies them. Clarity, working memory and attention at the end of a day are parameters I do not know precisely about myself and cannot predict. To be reasonably sure of applying the countermeasures I therefore have to monitor those too — that is, to add a second layer of monitoring, this time on myself.

The effort does not add up. It multiplies. And the result is a working condition with stressors on several fronts, some of which come from the instrument that was meant to relieve a few of them.

Worth saying that the session all this comes from had the best conditions available, namely someone who knew what to look for, was actively looking, and already had a list in hand. Under ordinary conditions, nothing that got documented would have been noticed.

## A note on what repeats

Two of the phenomena I met in this session I had already described months ago in another piece in this series: a measuring instrument sliding into an admission criterion, and a definition coming back as though it were a result. I knew them, I had written down how to recognise them, and I was watching for them.

They happened anyway. Which is the only thing here that comes close to being data: a phenomenon that recurs unchanged in front of someone lying in wait for it is not an anecdote told twice.

One question remains unresolved, and it concerns the economics of the whole business. If the only effective countermeasures are the ones demanding constant, non-delegable attention, the question is not how good they are. It is which kinds of work they are worth their price for, and which are better done another way.
